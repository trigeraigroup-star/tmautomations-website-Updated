# TMAutomations scroll website — approved design

Approved in the originating conversation; implementation authorized without a further approval round. This supersedes the August homepage direction, while preserving project tooling and unrelated files.

## Purpose and voice
Small-business owners should recognize their daily coordination burden, discover practical AI opportunities, and start a low-pressure conversation. Recover time, improve accuracy, and create income opportunities. Positioning: “We work alongside your team and stay accountable through implementation, adoption, and support.” No pricing, invented results, clients, or testimonials.

## Visual system
Editorial modern sans, cream paper, charcoal, natural wood, muted sage, restrained teal. TM in the wordmark is teal. Tactile human imagery sits on disciplined geometry with aligned, consistent workflow nodes. A thin teal thread connects the story within its artwork. No robots, neon, generic SaaS card grid, or video frame sequences. Lightweight SVG and HTML diagrams; sparse optimized human artwork.

## Narrative
1. Curiosity: “What if the work kept moving?” / “Without you pushing every step.” Provisional editable hero; incomplete workflow and faint office fragments.
2. Pressure: “You know this day. You live it.” Unread message, invoice, quote, team question, recognizable Zoom meeting/countdown accumulate around a tangled thread. Growth work is displaced.
3. Possibility: “It doesn’t have to stay this way.” Objects settle into orderly work.
4. Opportunity: “Saving time is only the beginning.” Balanced branches for better operations (time, coordination, accuracy) and growth (exposure, marketing, lead capture, follow-up/conversion). “AI can improve how the business operates—and create new systems that help it grow.”
5. Approach anchor: “The right solution begins with understanding the work.” Peers collaborate around the workflow; thread acts as pencil stroke. Include accountability sentence.
6. Process anchor: “How we work.” Pinned desktop section, three scroll-driven/clickable keyboard-accessible stages: Discover & Prioritize (map people, systems, bottlenecks, rank value/return), Build & Test (longest interval; assemble, test exceptions, correct, confirm), Integrate, Support & Improve (connect, train, adoption, issues, feedback). Clicking reveals immediately without scrolling. Mobile and no-JS show all stages.
7. Transformation: “From manual coordination to intelligent flow.” Symmetric manual inputs, central decision and human-control nodes, evenly spaced outputs. Main pinned spectacle. “Automation handles the repetitive coordination. Your people retain judgment and control.”
8. Work anchor: “See what moved. Know what needs attention.” Completed, active, and human-attention work are clearly distinguished. Honest illustrative workflow, no fabricated live stats. Empty case-study/testimonial data structures ready for real material.
9. Payoff: “The workday ends when it should.” / “Your business keeps moving—without asking you to carry every step.” Owner leaves into warm light; workflow remains controlled. Second beat: “Tell us how your business works today. We’ll start there.” / “Start a conversation.”
10. Contact anchor: name, email, optional phone, company, workflow challenge. TMAutomations; www.TMAutomations.io; (850) 775-6906 with tel link; explicitly editable address placeholder.
